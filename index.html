How it works

Set it up once. Students click a link.

Step 1

You set it up

Type the problem, add your rubric, and pick a time limit. chalkcast gives you a link and ready-made instructions to paste into your course.

Step 2

Students teach it

They open the link, draw on the whiteboard, and explain out loud on camera. There are no accounts and nothing to install.

Step 3

You grade it

Students upload the video and a whiteboard PDF to your course site, such as Canvas, and you score them with your rubric.

Built in

Two tools that save you time

Question bank

Choose a ready-made question by competency, either multiple choice or a draw to know it prompt. Each comes with a starter rubric you can edit. The bank starts with human anatomy.

Assignment defense

Ask one student a follow-up question about work they turned in. The question stays hidden until they press Begin Defense, and recording starts at the same moment.

Questions

What instructors ask first

Who is chalkcast for?

Instructors who ask students to explain how they got an answer. It was built for college anatomy and physiology, and it also fits nursing, chemistry, math, and high school science.

How do students submit assignments?

When they finish recording, students download a video and a PDF of their whiteboard and upload both to your course site, such as Canvas. chalkcast has no student accounts and stores nothing, so the files stay on the student's own device until they upload them.

Do students need an account or an app?

No. They click the link in your assignment and record in the browser on a computer with a camera and a microphone.

What is in the demo?

A sample student recording, then the whiteboard with a heart question loaded and a one-minute recording of your own. It is the same screen your students would use.
